MorningSingapore → Kansai
SQ620 departs Changi at 08:40

Board Singapore Airlines SQ620 from Terminal 3. 7.5-hour flight to Kansai International. Land at 16:10 local time.

Afternoon16:10 → 19:30
KIX Immigration, Customs & Haruka Express → Hotel

Land at 16:10. For a group of 11, allow a full 90 minutes for immigration, baggage claim, and customs — realistically clearing the terminal by ~17:30–17:45. Board the next Haruka Limited Express (~17:46 or 18:16 departure; trains run every 30 min). Arrive Kyoto Station ~18:55–19:30. Taxi convoy to Celestine Gion (~15 min). Check in, drop bags, and take a breath — you've made it.

Evening19:30 – 21:00
Gion Evening Stroll

After checking in, a short stroll through Gion's lantern-lit lanes. The prime maiko-spotting hour (Hanamikoji-dori, 17:30–18:30) will have passed, but ochaya engagements continue well into the night — patient observers still spot maiko and geiko heading between appointments until 21:00 and beyond. Stand quietly to the side; never approach or touch. The Shirakawa canal path is equally beautiful at night beneath the weeping willows.

Light Bite
Airport or en route

KIX has excellent terminal dining — ramen, udon, or a quality convenience store bento are all fine options before or after customs.

Dinner
Pontocho Alley
Various  ·  Izakaya  ·  Kaiseki  ·  River Terrace

The narrow lantern-lit alley along the Kamo River houses dozens of restaurants at every price point. In summer, magical "kawayuka" elevated riverside platforms open. Let the group explore freely — Uozane (grilled fish) or Nishiki Warai (yakitori) are excellent choices.

After Dinner
Bar Yoramu (バー ヨルアム)
Japan's only Israeli-Japanese Sake Bar  ·  Near Gion

100+ sake varieties, English guidance from sommelier Yoram Ofer. Tucked upstairs in a Gion backstreet. Reserve 1–2 days ahead.

Kyoto
👋 Yipor joins today

TransportMorning
🚶 Walk — Celestine Gion → Nishiki Market
~10 min on foot through Shijo-dori · Or taxi ¥800 · Nishiki runs parallel to Shijo between Teramachi and Takakura
Morning09:00 – 12:00
Nishiki Market — Kyoto's Kitchen
400-year-old covered market  ·  130 stalls  ·  Free to browse

A narrow 400m covered arcade lined with over 130 stalls — pickled vegetables, fresh tofu, grilled skewers, matcha sweets, knives, and Kyoto ceramics. Let everyone graze for breakfast as you walk: tamagoyaki on a stick, yudofu, warabi mochi, and freshly-fried kushikatsu. The older girls will love the ceramics and textile shops at the Teramachi end. Allow 2–3 hours to wander at leisure.

Lunch12:00 – 13:30
Nishiki Market Stalls & Teramachi
Street lunch  ·  Graze as you go  ·  Or sit-down at Ganko Sushi nearby

The market itself is the best lunch — grilled scallops, crab croquettes, and fresh sashimi on rice. If the group wants a sit-down, Ganko Nijo-en on the Kamo River or Mishima-tei (famous Kyoto beef) are nearby. Allow extra time for shopping — Teramachi is lined with antique shops, stationers, and tea merchants.

TransportAfternoon
🚕 Taxi — Nishiki area → Kiyomizudera
~10 min · ¥1,000 · Or city bus #206 from Shijo-Kawaramachi, ¥230 · Stroller accessible
Afternoon14:00 – 18:00
Kiyomizudera Temple
UNESCO World Heritage  ·  ¥500 entry  ·  Hillside views over Kyoto

Kyoto's most dramatic temple — a wooden stage jutting out over the hillside with panoramic city views. Then descend through the iconic cobbled lanes: Ninenzaka (二年坂) and Sannenzaka (三年坂), two of Japan's most photographed preserved streetscapes, lined with pottery shops, matcha cafés, and souvenir stalls. Allow 3 hours to meander down. Stroller manageable on the wider paths; some stone steps.

Late Afternoon17:00 – 18:30
Yasaka Shrine & Maruyama Park
Free entry  ·  10 min walk from Ninenzaka

A gentle end to the day — Yasaka Shrine's orange torii frame the entrance to Maruyama Park, Kyoto's most beloved public garden. Relax on the lawns before heading to dinner.

Afternoon Option17:30 – 18:30
Rest at Hotel Before Dinner
Celestine Gion  ·  15 min walk from Ninenzaka  ·  Ideal for Yeye & Grandmama

After a full day on foot at Nishiki Market and Kiyomizudera, anyone who needs to recharge is warmly encouraged to skip Maruyama Park and head back to Celestine Gion for an hour's rest before dinner. It is a simple 15-minute walk from Ninenzaka — or a short taxi if legs are tired. The rest of the group can linger in the park and meet at the restaurant. No obligation to keep walking.

Dinner
Muromachi Kaji (室町 かじ)
Bib Gourmand  ·  Kaiseki  ·  Kyoto Cuisine

One of Kyoto's celebrated Bib Gourmand listings — refined kaiseki at accessible prices. Seasonal small plates in the Muromachi tradition: dashi-based soups, grilled seasonal fish, tofu in many forms. Book well in advance. Adults and older children will love this.

After Dinner
Gion Finlandia Bar (ギオン フィンランディア)
Hidden Bar  ·  Machiya Townhouse  ·  Whisky & Cocktails

A beautifully preserved machiya townhouse with only 8 seats. Outstanding whisky selection including rare Japanese expressions. Zero signage — look for the small lantern. Reservations essential. Adults only after the children are in for the night.

Arashiyama, Ryoan-ji & Kinkaku-ji
Kyoto
TransportMorning
🚂 JR Sagano Line — Kyoto Stn → Saga-Arashiyama
~15 min · ¥240/person · Dep. Platform 31/32 · If doing the Hozugawa Cruise (see Morning Option below), continue one stop to Kameoka (~25 min total)
Morning08:00 – 10:00
Arashiyama Bamboo Grove
Forest Path  ·  Free Entry  ·  Taxi 30 min

Arrive before 9am for the iconic bamboo grove without crowds. The towering green stalks and filtered morning light are extraordinary. Rickshaws (jinrikisha) available nearby — a delight for Grandmama & Yeye.

Morning Option09:00 – 11:30
Hozugawa River Cruise — Kameoka to Arashiyama
Scenic Gorge Cruise  ·  ¥4,100 adult / ¥2,700 child (6–11)  ·  Book ahead  ·  Departs 09:00

Instead of the bamboo grove first, continue one stop on the JR Sagano Line to Kameoka (25 min from Kyoto Station) and board a traditional wooden boat through the dramatic Hozukyo gorge. Two hours of mountain scenery, river rapids, and cedar forest — the boat arrives naturally at Arashiyama's Togetsu-kyo bridge, placing you right at the bamboo grove by midday. Book at hozugawakudari.jp — departures at 09:00, 10:00, 11:00 (last at 15:00). Ideal for Grandmama & Yeye: seated the entire time, no hills, spectacular scenery. If doing this cruise, skip the early bamboo grove; it will be midday-busy by the time you arrive, but still worth a short walk-through before Tenryu-ji.

Morning10:00 – 12:30
Tenryu-ji Garden & Togetsukyo Bridge
UNESCO World Heritage  ·  Zen Garden

Tenryu-ji's famous garden frames Arashiyama mountain as its backdrop — a masterpiece of borrowed scenery. The flat garden paths are stroller and wheelchair accessible. Then stroll the riverside and cross the iconic Togetsukyo moon-crossing bridge.

Lunch12:00 – 13:00
Shoraian or Arashiyama riverside
Traditional restaurant  ·  Tofu cuisine  ·  Yudofu

Kyoto is famous for yudofu (simmered tofu) and Arashiyama has superb options along the Oi River. Shoraian serves classic Kyoto tofu sets in a historic riverside setting.

Transport~13:00 → 13:30
🚕 Taxi Convoy — Arashiyama → Ryoan-ji
~25 min · ¥2,200 per taxi · Book 2–3 taxis (pre-arrange at Arashiyama lunch restaurant) · No direct bus; taxi is the practical choice for a group of this size
Afternoon13:30 – 15:00
Ryoan-ji — The Rock Garden
UNESCO World Heritage  ·  ¥600 entry  ·  Closes 17:30 (Jun)  ·  Taxi 25 min from Arashiyama

The world's most celebrated Zen rock garden — fifteen stones arranged in raked white gravel, never all visible at once. The surrounding pond circuit (Kyoyochi) is flat and fully accessible, and the wooded grounds are serene even in peak season. Allow 1–1.5 hrs. Closes 17:30 in June; no rush.

Transport~15:00 → 15:15
🚕 Taxi — Ryoan-ji → Kinkaku-ji
~10 min · ¥900 per taxi · The two temples are neighbours in NW Kyoto — easiest short hop of the day
Afternoon15:15 – 17:00
Kinkaku-ji — The Golden Pavilion
UNESCO World Heritage  ·  ¥500 entry  ·  Closes 17:00 sharp  ·  Taxi 10 min from Ryoan-ji

Japan's most photographed building shimmers gold over a mirror-still pond. Afternoon light turns the gilded pavilion warm — the reflection in the pond is utterly breathtaking. The one-way garden walk (30–40 min) is flat and fully accessible. Plan to exit by 16:50; gates close at 17:00.

Dinner19:00
Pontocho Izumoya & Unagi Evening
Bib Gourmand  ·  Unagi (Eel) Specialist  ·  Pontocho

Izumoya is one of Pontocho's most celebrated unagi (freshwater eel) restaurants. Kabayaki-style grilled eel over rice — a quintessential Japanese dining experience. The restaurant has been serving this dish for generations. Book well ahead; request a kawayuka riverside terrace table if available in June.

After Dinner
Bar K6 (バーK6)
Classic Japanese Bar  ·  Kyoto  ·  Japanese Whisky

A Kyoto institution. Quiet, refined, serious about spirits. Aged Japanese whisky and masterful cocktails in a calm mahogany setting. The antithesis of trendy — and all the better for it.

→  Hotel → Arashiyama: Taxi ~¥3,000 (30 min)  ·  Arashiyama → Ryoan-ji: Taxi ~¥2,200 (25 min)  ·  Ryoan-ji → Kinkaku-ji: Taxi ~¥900 (10 min)  ·  Return to hotel ~19:30 before dinner: Taxi ~¥1,500

Morning10:00 – 11:30
Sunday Mass — Kawaramachi Catholic Church
カトリック河原町教会  ·  Sunday 10:30 Mass  ·  Japanese

A non-negotiable and deeply meaningful part of Sunday. The Kawaramachi Church (founded 1877) is a beautiful Gothic-influenced building in central Kyoto. Sunday morning Mass at 10:30 is held in Japanese — a moving experience in a foreign country. Arrive by 10:15. The church is near Shijo-Kawaramachi, Kyoto's main shopping district.

Transport~12:00
🚕 Taxi — Kawaramachi → Toji Temple
~20 min · ¥2,000 · Depart after a quick bite near the church
Lunch11:30 – 12:30
Shijo-Kawaramachi Area
Central Kyoto dining  ·  Steps from the church

The Shijo-Kawaramachi junction is surrounded by excellent restaurants — Katsukura (tonkatsu), Musashi (conveyor belt sushi), or the basement food hall of Takashimaya department store. A quick, relaxed Sunday lunch before heading to the market.

Afternoon12:30 – 16:00
Toji Temple — Kobo-san Flea Market (弘法市)
Held on the 21st of every month  ·  Free entry  ·  1,200 stalls  ·  Open until ~16:30

The largest monthly market in Japan — 1,200 stalls fill the grounds of Toji Temple on the 21st of every month. Antiques, vintage kimono, ceramics, hand-forged knives, food stalls, and extraordinary bric-à-brac from across Japan. The market runs until about 16:30, so a full afternoon is possible. Morning shoppers will have taken first pick, but the food stalls, ceramics, and textile vendors are excellent all afternoon. Budget for purchases — cash preferred. Grandmama & Yeye will love the unhurried pace; the girls will find vintage silk obi.

Late Afternoon16:30 – 18:30
Return to Hotel & Rest
Back to Celestine Gion  ·  Last group dinner with Yipor · she departs Tue 23 Jun from KIX

Return to Celestine Gion by taxi for a rest before dinner. Tonight is Yipor's last dinner with the full group — she stays on in Kyoto until her flight home on Tuesday 23 June (SQ621, 17:25 from KIX). A wonderful send-off before the group heads to Tokyo tomorrow.

Afternoon Option16:30 – 18:30
Kyoto Station Area Shopping
Toji is 5 min from Kyoto Station  ·  Skip the hotel, shop on the way back

Toji Temple is a 5-minute taxi from Kyoto Station — a natural detour on the way home. Isetan Kyoto B1 basement is exceptional for edible souvenirs: Kyoto pickles, wagashi, dashi, and matcha confections in beautiful packaging. The Cube (inside Kyoto Station) has a strong selection of Kyoto-branded ceramics, crafts, and gifts. Isetan Kyoto has 8 floors including fashion, homewares, and a rooftop café with views over the city. Those who prefer to rest can head straight back to the hotel — split the group freely at Toji.

🧳 To-Do TonightBefore Dinner
Arrange Yamato TA-Q-BIN Luggage Transfer → Hoshinoya Tokyo
Yamato Transport (宅急便)  ·  Celestine Gion → Hoshinoya Tokyo  ·  Overnight Delivery

Do this before dinner tonight — ask the Celestine Gion concierge to arrange a TA-Q-BIN hotel-to-hotel pickup. Here is exactly how it works:

  1. Go to the hotel front desk and request a takkyubin waybill (宅配伝票).
  2. Fill in destination: Hoshinoya Tokyo, 1-9-1 Otemachi, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo 100-0004  ·  Tel: 03-3222-1155.
  3. Write your name and check-in date (22 June) on the waybill so Hoshinoya staff can match the bags on arrival.
  4. Decide which bags to send — keep one small bag per person for the morning (Fushimi Inari clothes, toiletries, valuables, travel documents).
  5. Leave the bags with the concierge tonight. Yamato collects early the next morning; bags arrive at Hoshinoya Tokyo by the afternoon of 22 June — ready and waiting at check-in.

Cost: approx. ¥2,000–3,500 per large bag depending on size & weight. The concierge will complete the paperwork and arrange collection — you just need to hand over the bags and pay.

If a planned activity doesn't suit the mood, the weather, or the energy levels on the day — these are the best substitutes. All can be arranged spontaneously; none require advance booking unless noted.

Day 1 Thu 18 Jun · Arrival

Instead of: Gion evening stroll

Day 2 Fri 19 Jun · Nishiki + Kiyomizudera

Instead of: Kiyomizudera + Ninenzaka

  • Philosopher's Path (哲学の道) — a flat 2km canal walk from Ginkaku-ji (Silver Pavilion) to Nanzen-ji; entirely stroller-accessible and far gentler than Kiyomizudera's stone steps. Allow 2–3 hours.
  • Ginkaku-ji (Silver Pavilion) — UNESCO moss garden and sand sculptures; the ¥500 entry is one of Kyoto's best-value gardens. 20 min taxi from hotel.
  • Rest at hotel — skip the afternoon entirely; this option is already noted in the main schedule above.
Day 3 Sat 20 Jun · Arashiyama + Ryoan-ji + Kinkaku-ji

Instead of: Toji Flea Market afternoon

Instead of: Meiji Shrine / Cat Street

09Appendix
Practical Notes

Money & Payments

Japan remains heavily cash-based. Withdraw yen at 7-Eleven ATMs (accepts international cards, 24 hours). IC cards (Suica/ICOCA) work on all trains and many convenience stores. Tipping is not practised — it can cause offence.

Connectivity

Pre-order a pocket Wi-Fi device or eSIM before departure. IIJmio and JCOM Mobile offer reliable coverage. Both hotels have strong Wi-Fi. Suica cards top up via IC Card Reader app.

Accessibility

Japan has excellent accessibility infrastructure. Kyoto Station and Tokyo Station have lifts throughout. Most major temple grounds have flat, wide paths suitable for strollers and wheelchairs. Celestine Gion and both Tokyo hotels are lift-equipped. Staff are invariably helpful.

Health & Safety

Japan is extremely safe. Tap water is drinkable everywhere. Pharmacies (薬局 yakkyoku) are common — Matsumoto Kiyoshi is the main chain. Emergency: Police 110, Ambulance/Fire 119. Most hotels can connect you to an English-speaking medical service.

Etiquette Reminders

  • Remove shoes when entering homes, many traditional restaurants, and some temples
  • Eat and drink only in designated areas — no walking and eating
  • Speak quietly on public transport; phones on silent
  • Two hands when giving or receiving items, business cards, or money
  • Queues are sacred — always line up
  • Tattoos may restrict access to some onsen (hot springs)

Booking Reminders

  • Haruka Express: reserve seats in advance at KIX
  • Shinkansen: book Nozomi reserved seats (check JR Pass validity)
  • All dinners: confirm reservations 1–2 weeks before departure
